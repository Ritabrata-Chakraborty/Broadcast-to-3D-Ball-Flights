Abstract

The ball's 3D path underlies most soccer analytics, yet soccer has few monocular methods and little data: the Ball3D benchmark is, to our knowledge, the only public dataset with 3D ball ground truth over a whole match and its broadcast video, and its optimiser fits only the frames it sees. From one camera, a flight is fixed only up to its depth along the viewing rays; on the benchmark, removing the two contact anchors raises a physics fit's median broadcast error from 0.92 to 2.57 m, so the contacts carry most of the depth. We therefore learn a flight prior for where the data are weak. ArcPrior, a 1.24M-parameter transformer over per-frame world rays, predicts four flight hypotheses with spreads, which a batched Levenberg–Marquardt solver refines under the benchmark's losses and the spread-weighted prior. With oracle contacts (annotated times, ground-truth heights), ArcPrior lowers the median broadcast error of the benchmark's optimiser, Ball3D, from 0.955 to 0.848 m (pHolm = .001). It also forecasts a partially seen flight, at 3.72 against 6.27 m for a physics-only fit, with split-conformal 90 % error bounds that cover 89–91 % of test flights. From raw video with a YOLO11 ball detector and PnLCalib, the error rises to 1.68 m; fine-tuning only their heads brings it to 0.905 m with ground-truth-checked labels and to 0.968 m with labels from our own fits, which need no 3D trajectory ground truth. The median error of ArcPrior is below that of Ball3D in every scenario, significantly so with the benchmark's inputs and with off-the-shelf video. All results are cross-validated on one match with oracle contacts.

Method

Rays and ContactsOracle ContactsEach frame's camera and ball box give a ray through the bottom of the box and a box size. Every method in every scenario receives the same contacts: annotated contact times, contact type from the ground-truth height, and an anchor where the scenario's own ray reaches that height, on the pitch or at a player. The apex is never an input.
ArcPriorRay Tokens to HypothesesEach frame becomes one token; a learned query token pools the flight through six pre-norm transformer encoder layers, and an MLP maps it to K = 4 bounded flight hypotheses, each with spreads and a logit.
SolverRefinement and BoundsEach hypothesis is integrated by one batched, differentiable RK4 step per frame and refined by Levenberg–Marquardt under the benchmark's losses and the spread-weighted prior; the solver's covariance gives a per-flight predicted error Ω and split-conformal bounds.

Flight Model

A flight is the ball's motion between two contacts. It has ten parameters: start position and velocity, quadratic drag, lift and sidespin coefficients, and gravity. The benchmark's best monocular model, fitg, sets lift and sidespin to zero and fits gravity within 9.806 ± 3.5 m s−2, so within that range its image term alone cannot fix depth. ArcPrior fixes gravity and frees the lift and sidespin terms, so lift cannot be traded for gravity and curvature keeps a weak hold on depth.

ArcPrior

In the 1.24M-parameter network, each frame becomes one token in a flight-aligned frame, with its origin at the start anchor; rays remove the moving camera from what the network has to learn. Besides the ray geometry, a token holds the time from the start contact with Fourier features, the contact flags and end anchor, the log focal length, a size-based depth and the view. Time is never divided by the flight length, so a cut flight does not reveal its length.

Observability

At the solution, the Gauss–Newton covariance over the parameters that are not at a bound gives each flight a predicted error Ω. For split-conformal bounds we take, for each share of the flight seen, the 90 % quantile q of RMSE/Ω on the fold's validation block; the one-sided test bound is RMSE ≤ q Ω. ArcPrior-full is trained on whole flights and ArcPrior-cut on randomly cut flights.

Camera 06 films from behind a goal, and neither calibration network we tested recovers it (PnLCalib MV returns a camera on 51 % of its frames and is within 5 % of the true focal length on 3.5 %), so it is left out of every scenario to keep one test set. Flights are selected by their apex, the highest ground-truth point (at least 1 m); the apex is never an input. These airborne flights are a hard part of the benchmark: its shipped predictions reach a flight mAP of .312 / .235 (broadcast / static) on them, against the arc mAP of .397 / .307 that Ball3D reports over all of LP. E-full is the capped full-flight RMSE and E-part its mean over 30–90 % seen. Baselines: Ball3D, the optimiser of Ball3D with fitg on the same inputs and contacts; LM-fitg, our solver with the fitg model and start, no network; LM-lift, our solver with drag, lift and sidespin and fixed gravity, no network; SNv3D-size, per-frame depth from ball size (SoccerNet-v3D).
